Fintech · UPI · Trust & Safety Case study Case 01

Getting misdirected UPI money back, without giving up your privacy

You pay the wrong person by accident. Today there is no safe way to ask for it back. This flow fixes that without ever sharing a phone number.

Interactive demo temporarily offline — the full flow is written up below.
Where it breaks

UPI apps show you a verified name but never a number. That is deliberate privacy protection under India's DPDP rules. The trouble starts when a payment goes to the wrong but real person, because the same protection now leaves the sender with no way to reach them.

Banks and NPCI will not step in either. An authorised transfer to a valid ID is not fraud, so there is no dispute process. You have a name, maybe a photo, and no legal way to ask for your own money back.

Who feels it · Meera, 45, Pune

She paid ₹4,000 to a saved contact called "Plumber Ramesh", not knowing the number had been reassigned to a stranger months ago.

She wants her money back, but she refuses any workaround that hands her personal number to someone she does not know.

The fix: PhonePe acts as the go-between
  • Report. The sender taps "This wasn't who I meant to pay" right on the payment receipt.
  • Relay. PhonePe sends the recipient a fixed template message: "A sender thinks this ₹4,000 payment was a mistake." No numbers, no names.
  • Resolve. The recipient can refund in one tap through the same payment rails, or decline.
  • Guardrails. One template request per payment and no open chat, so it cannot be turned into harassment or a scam.
How I would measure itPilot: under ₹2,000, reviewed by hand
Disputes resolved without sharing any personal detailsMain metric
How often recipients respond to a requestSupporting
Time from report to refundSupporting
Fintech · Credit · Payments Live prototype Case 02

CRED: split a big card spend and let it settle itself

Big purchase on your card, then the awkward job of chasing friends for their share. This feature does the chasing for you and clears your bill as the money comes in.

Try the live prototype Opens in a new tab
▶ Tap to try
The real barrier

The hard part of group payments is not the money, it is how awkward it feels to ask for it. So this design takes the chaser and the debtor out of the equation and lets the system handle settlement quietly in the background.

Two people, one flow

The host wants the card points but hates asking friends for money. They need settlement that just happens and clears the bill.

The guest is happy to pay their share but forgets to check WhatsApp for the amount. They need a one-tap nudge, then pay and forget.

The flow: spot it, split it, collect it, credit it
  • Spot. CRED flags a large charge, say "Indigo Airlines, ₹45,000", and asks "Split this with your group?"
  • Split. You pick the contacts and set each person's share.
  • Collect. CRED sends each of them a UPI collect request.
  • Credit. As payments land they go straight onto that exact card charge. A bit of backend SQL matches each UPI payment to the right transaction.
How I would measure itReminders timed to the card due date
Credit health, keeping users under their 30% limitMain metric
Bills fully settled within 48 hoursPrimary
How often people open CRED each weekBusiness
Fintech · Payments · Retention Live prototype Case 03

Splitwise: close the gap between paying and marking it paid

People pay each other over UPI, then forget to open Splitwise and mark it settled. Balances drift, and that quietly pushes people off the app.

Try the live prototype Opens in a new tab
▶ Tap to try
The friction

Someone leaves Splitwise to pay over UPI, then never comes back to hit "Mark as Settled". Balances end up wrong, which causes real friction between friends and slowly kills the habit of using the app.

The idea · Smart-Sync

Smart-Sync watches for a UPI payment, through GPay or PhonePe deep-links, that matches an open expense. When it finds one, it shows a single "Confirm settlement?" prompt.

That turns a manual chore into something automatic, so the balances stay right in real time.

How I would measure itPitched as a Splitwise Pro upgrade
Settlement speed, how long a debt sits openPrimary
Whether accurate balances bring people back more oftenSecondary
Share of settlements via Smart-Sync vs. done by handGuardrail
Consumer · Discovery · Personalisation Figma prototype Case 04

Zomato: fixing the 6pm "what should I even eat" problem

You open a food app hungry, then scroll for twenty minutes and give up. You do not need another cuisine filter. You need something that gets what kind of day you are having.

Open the Figma mockup Interactive, opens in a new tab
▶ Tap to try
The real problem

Food apps work like search engines. They assume you already know what you want. But people do not order food by keyword, they order by mood and by whatever kind of day they have had.

The feature · a three-question flow

It reads the moment in three quick taps. Energy: celebrating, working, or wiped out. Risk: your usual comfort or something new. Portion: a light snack or a full feast.

What it should doCuts 1,000+ options down to about five good ones
Time to order, from fewer but better choicesPrimary
Retention, if it earns a spot as your go-to appSecondary
Mood data that makes recommendations sharper over timeLong term
